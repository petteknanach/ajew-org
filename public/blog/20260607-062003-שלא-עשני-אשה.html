שלא עשני אשה

A Deeper Understanding of the Morning Blessing

ב"ה

שלא עשני אשה. מספרים שאשת הנצי"ב שמעה איזה עם הארץ מברך "שלא עשני אשה" בכוונה גדולה, והתלוננה שהלוא היא הרבה יותר למדנית ממנו. והנצי"ב ענה: כל אחד מברך לעומת אשתו.

ונלע"ד: כך ענה לה לפייסה. אכן תשובתה בצדה — כי על זה גופא, שמפריע לה כל כך והרגישה נבזה ממעשה העם הארץ, על זה יש לברך "שלא עשני אשה".

English Translation & Commentary

They tell a story about the wife of the Netziv (R' Naftali Tzvi Yehudah Berlin, Rosh Yeshiva of Volozhin). She once heard a simple, unlearned man blessing "shelo asani isha" ("Who has not made me a woman") with great fervor and intention. She complained to her husband: "But I am far more learned than he is!" The Netziv answered: "Each person blesses relative to his own wife."

The author adds his own insight: The Netziv said this to placate her. But her complaint itself contains the answer — the very fact that it bothered her so deeply, that she felt devalued by the action of this ignoramus — this itself is reason to bless "shelo asani isha."

🧠 The Core Insight

This is a subtle and honest reflection. The Netziv's reply was a kind deflection — each man blesses in comparison to his own wife, and the Netziv's own wife was extraordinarily learned. But the author goes deeper: the emotional reaction itself reveals the blessing's truth.

A woman of the Netziv's wife's stature — learned, respected, partnered with one of the gedolei hador — was nevertheless wounded by the casual dismissal of a simple man. The vulnerability, the capacity to be stung by another's lack of recognition, the need for external validation — this is the very quality from which the blessing seeks protection.

The blessing is not about intellectual capacity or spiritual standing. It is about the particular nature of the feminine soul — more sensitive, more attuned to relationship, more vulnerable to being diminished. When the Netziv's wife felt hurt, she was experiencing the very quality that the blessing acknowledges.

The author's point: the Netziv was gentler than his wife realized. He wasn't denying her greatness. He was pointing out that the very thing she was upset about was the proof of what the blessing means.

Great blessings of Na Nach Nachma Nachman Me'Uman!
