June 8, 2026 📖 Morning Blessings — Likutay Moharan Torah 234

ממליכים את השם בברכת יוצר עם השם קמ"ה

Crowning the Divine Name Through the Yotzer Blessing

ב"ה

בברכה ראשונה של קריאת שמע בבוקר: מלאה הארץ קנינך, המלך המרומם.

בליקוטי מוהר"ן רל"ד: דע שיש שם שכשרוצין לעשות מלך — משתמשין בזה השם, והשם הוא קמ"ה וכו'.

וזה הראשי תיבות של: מלאה הארץ קנינך — ולכן מיד אחר כך: המלך.

English Translation & Commentary

In the first blessing before the morning Shema, we say: "מלאה הארץ קנינך" ("the earth is filled with Your acquisitions"), immediately followed by "המלך המרומם" ("the exalted King").

In Likutay Moharan Torah 234, Rabbainu reveals: "Know that there is a Name — when one wishes to make a king, they make use of this Name, and the Name is קמ"ה (Kamah)."

The initial letters of מלאה הארץ קנינך spell קמ"ה — the very Name that crowns kings. And therefore, immediately after this phrase, we say: המלך (the King)!

🧠 The Core Insight

This is a stunning liturgical discovery. The order of the blessing is not arbitrary — the words מלאה הארץ קנינך encode the Name קמ"ה in their initial letters, and only then do we declare המלך. The Name that crowns kings is invoked first, and then — through that Name — kingship is established.

This aligns with the deeper teaching of Torah 234: everything in creation is under the dominion of the Holy Names. To establish a king — whether the King of the Universe in our prayers, or the Tzaddik who rules through the Petek — one must invoke the Name through which kingship descends.

We literally crown Hashem as King every morning through this blessing, using the very mechanism described in Likutay Moharan: the Name קמ"ה, concealed in the words, activating the kingship of המלך.

And this Name itself — קמ"ה — equals 145, the gematria of the entire Petek when counted with the letters: Na Nach Nachma Nachman Me'Uman. The mouth of the mute is opened in song, crowning the King each morning anew.

Great blessings of Na Nach Nachma Nachman Me'Uman!
