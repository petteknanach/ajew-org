# Short Poetic Conclusion to Torahs 16-18

## Conclusion Poem for Torahs 16-18

תַּם חֵלֶק זֶה
מִשְּׁלֹשָׁה עֶשְׂרֵה
דִּבְרֵי פֶּלֶא
וּמַרְאֵה נוֹרָא
מִפִּי צַדִּיק
אוֹר גָּנוּז
הִלַּמְדָנוּ
דֶּרֶךְ חַיִּים
לָלֶכֶת בָּהּ
בְּתָמִים וּבֶאֱמֶת
בְּיִרְאָה וּבְאַהֲבָה
בְּשִׂמְחָה וּבְטוּב לֵבָב
בֶּאֱמוּנָה וּבִתְמִימוּת
בְּהִתְבּוֹנְנוּת וּבְהַ�ְׂכֵּל
בְּהִתְקָרְבוּת וּבְדִבּוּק
בְּהַעֲלָאָה וּבְהוֹרָדָה
בְּגִלּוּי וּבְהַסְתָּרָה
בְּתִקּוּן וּבְהַשְׁלָמָה
לִהְיוֹת כָּלֵב
בְּאֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל
לִרְאוֹת וּלְהַרְאוֹת
לְהוֹדִיעַ וּלְהִוָּדַע
לְהַמְשִׁיךְ וּלְהִמָּשֵׁךְ
לְהָאִיר וּלְהֵאָרוֹת
לִחְיוֹת וּלְהַחֲיוֹת
לִגְאֹל וּלְהִגָּאֵל
בְּרַחֲמִים וּבְאַהֲבָה
בְּשִׂמְחָה וּבְגִילָה
בִּישׁוּעָה וּבְרַחֲמִים
בְּמְהֵרָה בְּיָמֵינוּ
אָמֵן

## Translation

Completed is this section
Of thirteen
Words of wonder
And awesome vision
From the mouth of the tzaddik
Hidden light
Has taught us
A way of life
To walk in it
With wholesomeness and truth
With fear and love
With joy and good heart
With faith and simplicity
With contemplation and understanding
With drawing close and cleaving
With elevation and lowering
With revelation and concealment
With repair and completion
To be like Caleb
In the Land of Israel
To see and to show
To make known and to be known
To draw down and to be drawn
To illuminate and to be illuminated
To live and to give life
To redeem and to be redeemed
With mercy and love
With joy and gladness
With salvation and mercy
Speedily in our days
Amen

## Commentary

This poetic conclusion appears at the end of Torahs 16-18 (or in some editions, a section of 13 teachings). These teachings continue the foundational concepts introduced in the first section while exploring new dimensions.

### Key Themes from Torahs 16-18

Based on the poem's references, these teachings likely address:

1. **Vision and Revelation** - "Words of wonder and awesome vision"
2. **Hidden Light** - "Hidden light" suggests teachings about concealed aspects of Torah
3. **The Land of Israel** - Reference to Caleb in the Land of Israel indicates teachings about faith and connection to the Holy Land
4. **Transformation** - The poem emphasizes becoming ("To be like Caleb") rather than just knowing
5. **Reciprocal Relationship** - "To see and to show, to make known and to be known" suggests teachings about the dynamic relationship between teacher and student, or between God and humanity

### The Caleb Reference

The reference to Caleb is significant. In the Torah, Caleb was one of the twelve spies sent to scout the Land of Israel. While ten spies brought back a negative report, Caleb and Joshua maintained their faith, declaring: "We shall surely ascend and conquer it, for we can surely do it!" (Numbers 13:30).

In Breslov thought, Caleb represents:
- **Faith in the face of opposition** - Maintaining trust in God despite contrary evidence
- **Positive vision** - Seeing the potential rather than the obstacles
- **Courage** - Willingness to stand alone for truth
- **Connection to the Land** - Spiritual attachment to the Land of Israel

### Structural Elements

The poem follows a classic Hebrew poetic structure with parallel lines and thematic development:

1. **Acknowledgement** - Completing the section
2. **Source** - From the tzaddik, hidden light
3. **Teaching** - A way of life
4. **Qualities** - How to walk the path (wholesomeness, truth, fear, love, etc.)
5. **Practices** - What to do (contemplate, understand, draw close, cleave)
6. **Dynamics** - The spiritual processes (elevation/lowering, revelation/concealment)
7. **Goal** - Repair and completion
8. **Exemplar** - To be like Caleb in the Land
9. **Reciprocal Actions** - See/show, know/be known, etc.
10. **Ultimate Outcomes** - Live/give life, redeem/be redeemed
11. **Qualities of Redemption** - Mercy, love, joy, gladness, salvation
12. **Prayer** - Speedily in our days

### Liturgical Use

This poem would be recited after studying the corresponding section of Likutay Moharan. It serves to:
1. Summarize the key themes
2. Internalize the teachings as a "way of life"
3. Connect the teachings to biblical exemplars (Caleb)
4. Express the ultimate goals of the teachings
5. Pray for their implementation and fruition

### Contemporary Relevance

For modern students, this poem reminds us that studying Likutay Moharan is not just about acquiring knowledge but about:
1. **Transformation** - Becoming a different kind of person (like Caleb)
2. **Vision** - Developing spiritual sight
3. **Relationship** - Engaging in reciprocal spiritual dynamics
4. **Redemption** - Participating in personal and collective salvation

The emphasis on "to be like Caleb in the Land of Israel" suggests that these teachings help us develop the spiritual qualities needed to truly inhabit our spiritual inheritance, whether physically in the Land of Israel or spiritually in our relationship with God.
