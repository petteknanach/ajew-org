# Translation notes: Uzi Meshulam letter

## Alignment and editorial policy

- Paragraph numbers below refer to the source’s physical nonblank lines, treated as its paragraph units. The English draft preserves all 36 initial nonblank lines in order, then the source’s one blank line, then the final formula as the thirty-seventh nonblank paragraph.
- The Hebrew source was not emended. Bracketed material in the translation expands an acronym, supplies a technical gloss, identifies a citation, or flags unresolved source wording; it does not silently replace the source.
- Scriptural renderings follow the wording quoted by the document rather than normalizing it to a standard Bible translation. Polemical and hagiographic statements remain claims of the source/introduction.

## Ambiguous, corrupt, elliptical, or wordplay-dependent readings

1. **Paragraph 1 — ב״ה.** Expanded as “With the help of Heaven.” The abbreviation can also be expanded *barukh Hashem* (“Blessed is the Name”), but the documentary heading is commonly read *be-‘ezrat ha-Shem* / *be-‘ezrat shamayim* in function.
2. **Paragraph 2 — יש אומרים שהיה אחד מהל״ו צדיקים.** Rendered as the introductory claim “some say that he was one of the thirty-six righteous ones,” without endorsing its historicity. ל״ו is both the number thirty-six and the conventional designation of the hidden righteous.
3. **Paragraph 2 — סבא ישראל and הפתק הקדוש.** “Saba Yisrael” and “the holy note” are retained as insider Breslov designations. The referents are not identified in the source; they apparently denote Rabbi Yisrael Dov Odesser and the *petek*, respectively.
4. **Paragraph 2 — תלמידי היקר.** Rendered “My precious disciple.” “My dear disciple” is equally possible; “precious” retains the evaluative force of יקר.
5. **Paragraph 3 — ח״י במרחשוון.** Rendered as “the eighteenth of Marḥeshvan.” ח״י also literally spells “living/alive,” but the immediate calendrical construction makes the numeral primary; the possible “life” resonance was not inserted into the translation.
6. **Paragraph 4 — סימנו.** Rendered “Its sign is.” The antecedent may be the year, the letter, or a proposed identifying sign; the Hebrew does not specify it.
7. **Paragraph 4 — תהא שנת נקמת השי״ת.** The words spell out a phrase whose initials form תשנ״ה, the Hebrew year named in paragraph 3. This was preserved in Hebrew brackets after the English expansion. השי״ת is treated here as *ha-Shem yitbarakh*, “the Blessed Name.”
8. **Paragraph 4 — עזי / עוזי.** The quotation “My strength” (*‘uzzi*) also puns on the author’s name Uzi. The transliteration was included to retain the wordplay.
9. **Paragraph 5 — נחל נובע מקור חכמה.** Rendered literally and transliterated once. Its initials ננמ״ח recur explicitly in paragraph 35.
10. **Paragraph 6 — מג״ר דעתיק.** Expanded as “from the first three [sefirot] of ‘Atiq.” ג״ר is *gimel rishonot* (“the first three”), a kabbalistic technical abbreviation; “the first three of ‘Atiq” would be too opaque without the supplied *sefirot*.
11. **Paragraph 6 — מהאי רישא דלא אתיידע ולית מאן דקיימא בי׳.** The Aramaic is rendered “from that ‘Head that is Not Known,’ concerning which no man has any standing.” קיימא בי׳ is idiomatic and may instead mean “which no one apprehends/knows.” The translation preserves the ontological “standing” image.
12. **Paragraph 6 — לפני שמש ינון שמו / י, נון / מוח של שער הנו״ן.** The source dissects *Yinnon* as yod + *nun* and connects *nun* numerically with fifty and the Fiftieth Gate. “Mind/brain” was given for מוח because both physiological and noetic senses operate in the ensuing sexual-mystical argument.
13. **Paragraph 6 — וחמשים עלו.** The unpointed spelling quoted from Exodus 13:18 is conventionally vocalized *va-ḥamushim* (“armed/in ordered ranks”), but the author deliberately reads it *va-ḥamishim* (“and fifty”) to support the Fiftieth-Gate exposition. The draft therefore translates the author’s reading as “And fifty,” not the conventional biblical sense.
14. **Paragraph 6 — המיצרים / מצרים.** “Constraints” (*meitzarim*) and “Egypt” (*Mitzrayim*) form an explicit sound/etymological play. The transliteration of the former was supplied to keep the connection visible.
15. **Paragraph 6 — ערוות מצרים.** Rendered literally as “the nakedness of Egypt.” It can carry the broader sense of sexual shame/indecency; “nakedness” preserves the biblical-kabbalistic diction.
16. **Paragraph 6 — רוח אפינו / ניאוף.** אפינו (“our nostrils”) and ניאוף (“fornication”) contain the same Hebrew letters. The source’s syntax after the quotation—נגד ולשבר ולמגר אותיות—is elliptical. It was rendered as Messiah being “opposed to, and in order to break and eradicate, the letters” of *ni’uf*, and the anagram was explicitly identified.
17. **Paragraph 7 — דור הפלגה שהוא כפירות.** The plural כפירות is literally “heresies.” It was not smoothed into the singular “heresy.”
18. **Paragraph 7 — והוא גנוז בי׳ במשיח.** The phrase is syntactically and referentially unclear: בי׳ may mean “in Him,” “in it,” or preserve the divine letters Y-H; it may also participate in a hidden-letter claim involving משיח. The draft gives the deliberately literal “he is hidden in Y-H, in Messiah” and does not resolve the mechanism.
19. **Paragraph 7 — אם אין קמח אין תורה.** The mishnaic saying literally concerns flour/sustenance and Torah. Here it anticipates the gematria in paragraph 35. It was translated literally and not paraphrased as “without livelihood there is no Torah.”
20. **Paragraph 8 — אור מ״ה החדש.** Retained as “the light of the New MaH.” מ״ה is a technical kabbalistic divine-name configuration with numerical value 45; reducing it to “what” would destroy the technical sense.
21. **Paragraph 8 — ותנ׳ח׳מ׳ני.** The source inserts apostrophe-like marks inside “and You shall comfort me,” apparently to highlight letters associated with Naḥman. The exact segmentation is not explained. The English supplies the whole Hebrew form in brackets rather than inventing a definite acrostic.
22. **Paragraphs 8–9 — Isaiah 12 quotation.** The opening quotation mark occurs in paragraph 8 and the closing mark only at the end of paragraph 9. The English retains the cross-paragraph continuation. The source’s wording and punctuation are uneven and were not silently conformed to a critical biblical text.
23. **Paragraph 9 — ובתרגום שם.** Rendered “in the Targum there.” The source does not identify the Targum by name; “Targum Jonathan” was therefore not supplied in the draft.
24. **Paragraph 9 — הייחוד הפנימי.** Rendered “the inward unification.” “Inner union” is possible, but *yiḥud* is preserved in its technical kabbalistic sense rather than psychologized.
25. **Paragraph 10 — ההשלכות.** Rendered “castings-down,” literally preserving the unusual noun alongside descents and falls. “Rejections” or “being thrown about” may be intended.
26. **Paragraph 11 — the purported Targum and its quotation marks.** This Aramaic passage does not read as an unmodified standard Targum text; it includes the kabbalistic phrase לאורייתא דעתיקא סתימאה (“to the Torah of the Concealed Ancient One”) and additional material. The source opens quotation marks but supplies no visible closing quotation mark. It was translated as quoted, not corrected against a canonical Targum; the English closes the quotation at the paragraph end solely to delimit the quoted passage.
27. **Paragraph 11 — וכדי דתבית.** Rendered “and when I return.” The verbal person/form is unstable in the transmitted wording and could be construed differently; the first-person context of דחבית, מני, and אנא supports the chosen reading.
28. **Paragraph 11 — רבא קדישא אמר לאשראה.** The Aramaic is grammatically awkward. It was translated literally as “the Holy Great One said to cause … to dwell.” רבא קדישא could instead denote “the holy master,” and אמר might reflect a corrupt form with a future/intention sense.
29. **Paragraph 12 — בסה״ק.** Expanded as “in the holy books” (*ba-sefarim ha-kedoshim*). A singular “in the holy book” is formally possible, but the conventional abbreviation and unspecified source favor the plural.
30. **Paragraph 12 — אם תבוא לרדת.** Literally redundant (“if it should come to descend”); rendered “if it should come down,” preserving the hypothetical without reproducing unidiomatic redundancy.
31. **Paragraph 13 — קיצך המקטרג הגיע … עתה הקץ עליך המקטרג.** The sentence is abrupt and the second wording is the author’s adaptation addressed to the Accuser, not a verbatim continuation of Ezekiel 7. The draft preserves this as direct address rather than presenting the whole as canonical biblical text.
32. **Paragraph 16 — וידברו עליו … אשר לא יאומן כי יסופר.** Presented by the source in quotation marks without an attribution. It was retained as a quotation and not assigned to Rabbi Naḥman or another work without evidence.
33. **Paragraph 17 — עוזי אזולאי ב׳ר׳ דוד וציונה.** Preserved as “Uzi Azulai, son of Rabbi David and Tziona.” The final signature says “Uzi A. … Meshulam,” so אזולאי may be a middle name, patronymic element, scribal anomaly, or intentional alternate designation. It was not normalized to “Meshulam.” ב״ר was expanded as “son of Rabbi.”
34. **Paragraph 17 — ממתין בכליון נפש.** Rendered “wait with consuming longing,” preserving the devotional idiom of an intense, soul-consuming yearning without the misleading literal implication that the soul is physically expiring.
35. **Paragraphs 17–18 — לאקמא שכינתיה/שכינתא מעפרא.** Rendered “to raise His/the Shekhinah from the dust.” The shift between the pronominal Aramaic form and the absolute form is preserved in English where possible.
36. **Paragraph 18 — כדברך עשה ולא כבקשתי.** Rendered as an imperative contrast: “act according to Your word, and not according to my request.” The terse Hebrew omits an explicit subject and can also be read as a petition that events occur in accordance with the divine word.
37. **Paragraph 18 — אכי״ר.** Expanded as “Amen; so may it be His will” (*amen, ken yehi ratzon*).
38. **Paragraph 20 — Yedid Nefesh wording.** The source’s feminine references culminate in שפחת עולם, “everlasting handmaiden.” This was preserved rather than regularized to a familiar printed variant. The source does not name the hymn; the identification is editorial and is placed in brackets.
39. **Paragraph 20 — spaced בשמחת עולם.** The emphatic letter-spacing and exclamation were represented by spaced uppercase English: “W I T H  E V E R L A S T I N G  G L A D N E S S!”
40. **Paragraph 21 — אין בן דוד בא אלא בהיסח הדעת.** Rendered literally as a rabbinic claim about the Son of David coming through diversion of attention. The source then glosses this as already being present but concealed; the gloss remains the author’s, not part of the cited dictum.
41. **Paragraph 22 — יתלבש בכמה מיני לבושין.** The antecedent of “will be clothed” is unclear (the matter, the soul, or its concealment). The translation retains the impersonal “it,” avoiding an unsupported identification.
42. **Paragraph 22 — ולא יהיה אפשרות להתקרב לאנשים ולבית ישראל אל זאת הנשמה.** The syntax is irregular. It was rendered as “there will be no possibility for people and for the House of Israel to draw near to this soul,” taking לאנשים ולבית ישראל as the logical agents rather than objects.
43. **Paragraph 23 — הס״מ.** Preserved as “the Samekh-Mem,” the euphemistic letter-name designation for Samael, rather than expanding it to a supplied proper name the source avoids.
44. **Paragraph 23 — הוצאת ז״ל.** Expanded as “the emission of seed in vain” (*hotza’at zera‘ le-vatalah*). ז״ל elsewhere means “of blessed memory,” but in this sexual context it unambiguously abbreviates *zera‘ le-vatalah*.
45. **Paragraph 24 — מהתל בהם.** Rendered “makes sport of them.” “Deceives/mocks them” is also possible; the chosen phrase preserves the sense of derisive manipulation.
46. **Paragraph 25 — גדול / גדול ומקובל.** Quotation marks around “great one” and “great one and kabbalist” were retained because they signal the author’s polemical distancing. מקובל here means a practitioner/authority in Kabbalah, not merely an “accepted” person.
47. **Paragraph 27 — הכשר כלים.** Rendered and transliterated as *Hilkhot Hekhsher Kelim* [Laws of Rendering Vessels Fit]. The source calls it הלכה ד׳; no attempt was made to verify or correct the internal citation.
48. **Paragraph 27 — פלא יועץ / אלף אורות.** “Wondrous Counselor” preserves the Isaianic title and “the thousand lights” preserves the kabbalistic technical phrase. Neither was converted into a generalized description.
49. **Paragraph 27 — ביזיונות ושפיכות דמים.** The source couples humiliations with “shedding of blood,” an idiom also evoking public shaming. The draft keeps the literal blood language and then renders להתבייש as “be humiliated,” rather than silently replacing both with “embarrassment.”
50. **Paragraph 29 — the opening quotation.** A quotation mark opens before שמשבר but no clearly matching close appears before the subsequent parenthesis and discussion. The draft closes the translated proposition after “stumbling” for readability while recording here that the source punctuation is defective.
51. **Paragraph 29 — להתקרב (לא להכיר!).** Preserved as “to draw near (not to become acquainted!).” The parenthesis appears to insist on a distinction that sits uneasily with the later positive use of להתוודע (“become acquainted”). It was not harmonized.
52. **Paragraph 29 — וכך קורא למתקרבים.** Literally “and thus [he/it] calls to those drawing near,” which lacks a clear subject and is contextually awkward. It may be a corruption of וכך קורה (“and thus it happens”) or a different omitted-object construction. The draft uses the literal “And thus it calls …” rather than silently emending to “happens.”
53. **Paragraph 29 — תו ּכי / תוכי.** The typography splits the word *tukki* (“parrot”) as תו ּכי, apparently to enable a private or phonetic explanation. The draft preserves both the split form and normal Hebrew spelling. The author’s parenthetical identification of the “parrot” with Uzi A. Meshulam is translated as stated, without smoothing its self-deprecating first-person implication.
54. **Paragraph 29 — מד״ע.** This acronym or defective form could not be securely expanded from context. It is retained in Hebrew and explicitly marked “[an unresolved acronym]”; no conjectural source title was inserted.
55. **Paragraph 29 — נתהוית מפרק תחתון דהוד של אריך־אנפין.** Rendered technically as Malkhut “came into being from the lower segment of Hod of Arikh Anpin.” פרק can mean joint, section, or segment; “segment” best preserves the partzuf-anatomical claim without over-specifying a limb.
56. **Paragraph 30 — ומשם מלך המשיח.** Elliptical, with no finite verb. Rendered “and from there [comes] the King Messiah,” supplying only the minimum verb needed in English. “And from there is the King Messiah” is also possible.
57. **Paragraph 30 — וממנו ישיגו ויבינו החכמה.** The masculine antecedent of ממנו may be the King Messiah or a prior masculine mystical entity. The draft takes it as “from him,” while the reference remains contextually open.
58. **Paragraph 30 — אור הלבנה.** The source says “the light of the moon” with a feminine verb for elevation. The draft does not import the possible Malkhut symbolism into the main text.
59. **Paragraph 31 — הרוח הרביעי.** The source shifts from feminine רוח הרביעית in paragraph 30 to grammatically masculine הרביעי here. Both are translated “the fourth spirit”; the grammatical inconsistency is not doctrinally resolved.
60. **Paragraph 31 — תשווה עליו.** Rendered “You set upon him,” following the source’s quoted biblical sense. The ensuing gloss makes “him” the King Messiah; the translation retains that interpretive step as the author’s.
61. **Paragraph 32 — מצד שני … מצד שני, צד הסטרא אחרא.** The Hebrew repeats “on the other hand” and then “side of the Other Side.” The English deliberately preserves the repetition rather than stylistically smoothing it away.
62. **Paragraph 32 — שומע אותו מתקרב.** The word order is awkward. Rendered “as he draws near he hears,” taking אותו as referring back to the person drawing near rather than as a direct object.
63. **Paragraph 32 — כל אלו זה תוכחה.** Grammatically discordant (“all these—this is a rebuke”). Rendered “all these are a rebuke,” preserving the assertion while making the minimum number agreement required in English.
64. **Paragraph 32 — טיפי מוחו.** Rendered literally as “the drops of his brain,” retaining the source’s physiological-kabbalistic conception of semen rather than smoothing it into a general “mental blemish.”
65. **Paragraph 33 — לא היה נשמע לו.** Rendered “would not have been made audible to him.” The impersonal/passive construction is unusual; “he would not have heard” is idiomatically possible but less formally equivalent.
66. **Paragraph 33 — וכל והמריבות.** The duplicated conjunction/article is evidently defective. The draft renders the likely syntactic value, “and all the quarrels,” but does not treat this as evidence for additional content.
67. **Paragraph 35 — ננמ״ח = קמח.** ננמ״ח is the initialism of נחל נובע מקור חכמה (“flowing stream, source of wisdom”). Both ננמ״ח and קמח have standard gematria 148 (50+50+40+8 and 100+40+8). The equation and the literal “flour” were preserved explicitly.
68. **Paragraph 35 — מקור חכמה הקדומה.** The adjective הקדומה can modify “wisdom” (“primordial wisdom”) or, less naturally, the whole title/source. The draft chooses “source of primordial wisdom.”
69. **Paragraph 35 — ובזה עצמו.** Rendered “through this itself,” preserving the reflexive emphasis; the exact referent may be his recognition of the attempted distancing or the preceding identification of the righteous one.
70. **Paragraph 36 — הממתין לתלמידיו … שיגיעו בקרוב מאד.** The signature sentence is elliptical. The draft construes the author as “He who awaits his disciples” and שיגיעו as a wish “that they arrive very soon.” The phrase could also mean that the blessing should reach them very soon.
71. **Paragraph 36 — עוזי א. בר׳ דוד משולם.** Preserved as “Uzi A. son of Rabbi David Meshulam.” The abbreviated middle initial א. was not expanded conjecturally; it may correspond to אזולאי in paragraph 17, but the source does not explicitly say so.
72. **Final paragraph — נַ נַחְ נַחְמָ נַחְמָן מאומן.** Retained as the Breslov formula “Na Naḥ Naḥma Naḥman me-Uman,” transliterating rather than semantically translating the staged name formation.
