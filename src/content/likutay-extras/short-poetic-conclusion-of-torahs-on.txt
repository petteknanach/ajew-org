# Short Poetic Conclusion of Torahs

## General Conclusion Poem

כָּלָה הַדָּבָר
נִגְמַר הַסֵּפֶר
דִּבְרֵי אֱלֹקִים
חַיִּים וְקַיָּמִים
לְעוֹלְמֵי עַד
וּלְנֵצַח נְצָחִים
יְהִי רָצוֹן
שֶׁיִּהְיוּ לְרָצוֹן
דִּבְרֵי תוֹרָה זֹאת
לִפְנֵי שׁוֹכֵן מְעוֹנִים
וְיַעֲלֶה לְמַעְלָה
כְּעוֹלָה וּכְקָרְבָּן
לְכַפֵּר עַל עֲווֹנוֹת
וּלְטַהֵר נְפָשׁוֹת
לְהָאִיר עֵינַיִם
וּלְהַחֲיוֹת לֵבָב
לְהָשִׁיב שְׁבוּת
וּלְגָאֵל גְּאֻלָּה
לִבְנוֹת בַּיִת
וּלְהָקִים מִקְדָּשׁ
לְהָשִׁיב שְׁכִינָה
וּלְהַמְלִיךְ מֶלֶךְ
לְהָבִיא מָשִׁיחַ
וּלְתַקֵּן עוֹלָם
בְּמַלְכוּת שַׁדַּי
בִּמְהֵרָה בְיָמֵינוּ
אָמֵן

## Translation

Completed is the matter
Finished is the book
Words of God
Living and enduring
Forever and ever
And for eternity of eternities
May it be the will
That they be acceptable
These words of Torah
Before the Dweller in the heavens
And may they ascend on high
Like an offering and sacrifice
To atone for sins
And to purify souls
To illuminate eyes
And to revive hearts
To return captivity
And to redeem redemption
To build a house
And to establish a sanctuary
To return the Divine Presence
And to crown a king
To bring the Messiah
And to repair the world
In the kingdom of the Almighty
Speedily in our days
Amen

## Commentary

This general conclusion poem can be used at the end of any section or the complete work of Likutay Moharan. It serves as a closing prayer and blessing after studying the teachings.

### Structure and Themes

The poem moves through several thematic movements:

1. **Completion** - Acknowledging the completion of study
2. **Acceptance** - Praying that the study is accepted by God
3. **Atonement** - Asking that the study serve as atonement
4. **Illumination** - Requesting spiritual enlightenment
5. **Redemption** - Praying for personal and collective redemption
6. **Rebuilding** - Asking for the rebuilding of the Temple
7. **Messianic Hope** - Expressing hope for the coming of the Messiah
8. **World Repair** - Praying for the repair of the entire world

### Liturgical Use

This poem can be used in several contexts:

1. **After completing a section** of Likutay Moharan
2. **After a study session** as a closing prayer
3. **On special occasions** when completing the entire work
4. **As a personal meditation** on the goals of Torah study

### Spiritual Significance

The poem expresses the Breslov understanding of Torah study as not just an intellectual exercise but a transformative practice with cosmic implications. Through studying Rabbi Nachman's teachings, we participate in:

1. **Personal transformation** - Purification of the soul, illumination of understanding
2. **Collective redemption** - Return from exile, rebuilding of the Temple
3. **Cosmic repair** - Restoration of the Divine Presence, coming of the Messiah

### Connection to Traditional Prayers

The language and themes echo traditional Jewish prayers, particularly:
- The prayers after reading from the Torah
- The closing prayers of various liturgical services
- Messianic prayers and hymns

This connection situates Likutay Moharan within the broader tradition of Jewish learning while emphasizing its unique contribution.

### Practical Application

When reciting this poem, one might reflect on:

1. **What have I learned?** - Reviewing the key insights from the study session
2. **How have I changed?** - Considering personal transformation through study
3. **What will I implement?** - Identifying practical applications of the teachings
4. **How does this connect to redemption?** - Seeing personal growth as part of cosmic repair

The poem thus serves as a bridge between the intellectual content of the teachings and their practical, transformative, and redemptive implications.
