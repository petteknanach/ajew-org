# Short Poetic Preface for Torahs 1-15

## Preface Poem for First Section

בְּשֵׁם ה' אֵל עוֹלָם
אֲשֶׁר בָּרָא עוֹלָמוֹ
בְּחָכְמָה וּבִתְבוּנָה
בְּדַעַת וּבְהַשְׂכֵּל
וְנָתַן לָנוּ תּוֹרָתוֹ
אוֹרָהּ כְּאוֹר יוֹם
פְּנִינָהּ כְּפְנִינֵי מַרְגָּלִיּוֹת
דְּרָכֶיהָ דַרְכֵי נֹעַם
וְכָל נְתִיבוֹתֶיהָ שָׁלוֹם

וְעַתָּה בָּאִים אֲנַחְנוּ
לִכְתֹּב וּלְחַבֵּר
לִקּוּטֵי מוֹהֲרַ"ן
דִּבְרֵי אֱלֹקִים חַיִּים
מִפִּי צַדִּיק
אוֹר הָאוֹרוֹת
אֲשֶׁר הֵאִיר לָנוּ
בְּדֶרֶךְ נָכוֹן
לָלֶכֶת בּוֹ
לִפְנֵי ה'
בְּתָמִים וּבְיֹשֶׁר
בְּשִׂמְחָה וּבְטוּב לֵב
בֶּאֱמוּנָה וּבִתְמִימוּת

וְזֶה חֵלֶק רִאשׁוֹן
מִשִּׁבְעָה עָשָׂר
דִּבְרֵי נֹעַם
וּמַתַּת חֵן
יְהִי רָצוֹן
שֶׁיִּהְיוּ לְרָצוֹן
לִפְנֵי שׁוֹכֵן מְעוֹנִים
וְיַעֲלוּ לְמַעְלָה
כְּעוֹלָה וּכְקָרְבָּן
לְכַפֵּר עַל עֲווֹנוֹת
וּלְטַהֵר נְפָשׁוֹת
לְהָאִיר עֵינַיִם
וּלְהַחֲיוֹת לֵבָב
לִישׁוּעָה וּלְרַחֲמִים
לְחַיִּים טוֹבִים
וּלְשָׁלוֹם
בְּמְהֵרָה בְּיָמֵינוּ
אָמֵן

## Translation

In the name of God, Eternal Lord
Who created His world
With wisdom and understanding
With knowledge and intelligence
And gave us His Torah
Its light like the light of day
Its pearl like precious pearls
Its ways are ways of pleasantness
And all its paths are peace

And now we come
To write and compose
Likutey Moharan
Words of the living God
From the mouth of the tzaddik
Light of lights
Who illuminated for us
A straight path
To walk in it
Before God
With wholesomeness and uprightness
With joy and good heart
With faith and simplicity

And this is the first part
Of seventeen
Words of pleasantness
And gift of grace
May it be the will
That they be acceptable
Before the Dweller in the heavens
And may they ascend on high
Like an offering and sacrifice
To atone for sins
And to purify souls
To illuminate eyes
And to revive hearts
For salvation and mercy
For good life
And for peace
Speedily in our days
Amen

## Commentary

This poetic preface introduces the first section of Likutay Moharan (Torahs 1-15 or 1-17, depending on the edition). It sets the spiritual tone and intention for studying these teachings.

### Structure and Themes

The poem has three main sections:

1. **Invocation** - Beginning in God's name, acknowledging God as Creator and Giver of Torah
2. **Introduction** - Presenting Likutay Moharan as "words of the living God" from the tzaddik
3. **Prayer** - Asking for the teachings to be accepted and effective

### Key Elements

#### 1. The Source of Torah
The poem emphasizes that Torah comes from God ("Who gave us His Torah") but is transmitted through the tzaddik ("From the mouth of the tzaddik"). This reflects the Breslov understanding that true Torah requires both divine origin and human transmission through righteous channels.

#### 2. The Nature of Torah
Using imagery from traditional Jewish sources:
- "Light like the light of day" - Torah illuminates like sunlight
- "Pearl like precious pearls" - Torah is valuable and beautiful
- "Ways of pleasantness" - From Proverbs 3:17
- "All its paths are peace" - Also from Proverbs 3:17

#### 3. The Purpose of Study
The poem lists several purposes for studying Likutay Moharan:
- To walk before God with specific qualities (wholesomeness, uprightness, joy, faith, simplicity)
- To achieve spiritual effects (atonement, purification, illumination, revival)
- To bring practical benefits (salvation, mercy, good life, peace)

### Liturgical Function

This preface serves several functions:

1. **Kavannah (Intention)** - Sets the proper mindset for study
2. **Blessing** - Invokes divine assistance for understanding
3. **Framework** - Places Likutay Moharan within the broader tradition of Torah study
4. **Aspiration** - Expresses the hoped-for outcomes of study

### Historical Context

Such poetic prefaces were common in traditional Jewish works, particularly in Hasidic literature. They served to:
1. Establish the work's legitimacy
2. Connect it to the chain of tradition
3. Express the author's humility and devotion
4. Invoke divine blessing for the endeavor

### Breslov Specific Elements

While using traditional forms, the poem incorporates distinct Breslov themes:

1. **The Tzaddik** - Emphasis on the tzaddik as conduit for divine wisdom
2. **Joy** - Mentioned as a key quality for serving God
3. **Simplicity** - Faith with simplicity (emunah peshutah) is a central Breslov value
4. **Transformation** - Study leads to personal transformation (purification, illumination, revival)

### Practical Use

When reciting this preface before studying the first section of Likutay Moharan, one might reflect on:

1. **Gratitude** - Thankfulness for having access to these teachings
2. **Humility** - Recognition of one's need for spiritual guidance
3. **Openness** - Willingness to be transformed by the teachings
4. **Connection** - Feeling linked to the chain of transmission from Rabbi Nachman

### Connection to the Conclusion Poem

This preface poem pairs with the conclusion poem for the same section (found in "short-poetic-conclusion-to-torahs-1-15.txt"). Together, they create a complete liturgical frame for studying this section:
- **Preface** - Sets intention before study
- **Study** - Engages with the teachings
- **Conclusion** - Integrates and prays for implementation

This framing practice helps transform Torah study from mere intellectual exercise into a holistic spiritual practice that engages mind, heart, and will.

For contemporary students, using such poetic frames can help create sacred space for study and deepen the transformative potential of engaging with Rabbi Nachman's teachings.
