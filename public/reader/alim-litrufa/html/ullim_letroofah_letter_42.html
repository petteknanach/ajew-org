Leaves for Healing
The Sacred Letters of Rebbe Nachman of Breslov and Reb Nussun of Breslov, of blessed memory
LETTER FORTY-TWO
Tuesday, Parshas P'kudai, 23rd of Adar  ·  Year 5591 (1831)
Written from Uman
To Peace and life — to my beloved, dear as my soul — the distinguished, veteran scholar, our Teacher the Rabbi Chaim Nachum — may his light shine forever.

At this very hour I heard that a letter arrived from you — that your father-in-law has passed away — may his soul be bound in the bond of life; may the Omnipresent comfort you. And from my love for you I hastened to write you these few lines — for thank G-d I arrived here safely yesterday — and blessed be Hashem all is well.

However — I was very displeased with you for having placed the burden of your sister upon the tzadekess [righteous woman] Marat Adl — may she live — and for having issued her a strict warning not to be married to a plain man until a lamdan [Torah scholar] specifically should come along. Is it proper to do such a thing? If you want to give her in marriage to a Torah scholar specifically — take her into your own home and let her be your responsibility and you can do as you wish. But how can you wish to place the burden upon Adl — may she live — who is in her own home, whose situation is very pressed as you know — and bind her from marrying until a Torah scholar specifically comes along?

And who knows whether the Torah scholar would even agree to marry her on the condition that he go door-to-door with her [in poverty] — as you spoke to her — even if your sister herself were willing for this. All the more so since in truth your sister is not at all willing for this. And if our Sages of blessed memory said [Pesachim 49a] — a man should always sell [all he has] and marry his daughter to a Torah scholar — but you have not sold anything at all, and you have nothing to sell. Why do you not remember the words of our Sages of blessed memory [ibid. 113a]: your daughter has come of age — free your slave and so forth? All the more so since you placed her in the home of the tzadekess Adl — may she live — without permission, and you are issuing such commands upon her.

You have acted as Esav [hiskalta aso — הִסְכַּלְתָּ עֲשׂוֹ: literally "you fooled yourself — Esav." The name Esav is invoked deliberately: just as Esav sold his birthright impulsively and rashly, forfeiting something precious for nothing, so too Chaim Nachum — through his hasty interference — caused a worthy match to be lost] — my dear brother — for a suitable shiduch [match] came her way, in my opinion — and because of your words it was disrupted. Although your sister herself was willing — the matter was nonetheless confused and undone through you — as I will tell you if G-d wills it. Therefore see to rectify this — and write to her at once that she should not be exacting specifically on this — only trust in Hashem Yisborach that He will guide her in the true path. More than this there is no need to extend on this matter.

Be strong and resolute, my brother and beloved student — cast upon Hashem your burden and He will sustain you [Tehillim 55:23] — and do not worry the worry of tomorrow. And let these matters be in your eyes every day as entirely new — so as not to think from one day to the next at all — only whatever your hand finds to do with your strength, do it — each and every day.

And be careful from now on to walk with what our Master, of blessed memory, wrote: Today — if you hearken to His voice [Tehillim 95:7] — today specifically. And although even this one day alone is hard to fully discharge one's daily obligation — join to it the other sichos [conversations] and the holy Torahs from the Nachal Novea M'kor Chochmah [נַחַל נוֹבֵעַ מְקוֹר חָכְמָה — the Likutay Moharan] — which are filled with very deep counsel — in particular the simple and straight path [haDerekh haTom v'haYoshor] on the verse I will sing to my G-d while I yet exist [Tehillim 146:2] and so forth. And this suffices for now.

The words of one who loves you truly, forever.

Nussun of Breslov.

Overview: A two-part letter to Chaim Nachum — written from Uman, at the tziyun of Rebbe Nachman. The first half is a pointed, Talmudically grounded rebuke: Chaim Nachum has placed an unreasonable idealistic demand on his unmarried sister, who is living under the care of the tzadekess Adl — and his interference has caused a worthy shiduch to fall through. Reb Nussun invokes the name of Esav — hiskalta aso — as a sharp biblical accusation: you have sold something precious rashly, as Esav sold his birthright. The Talmudic quotes (Pesachim 49a and 113a) are deployed with precision and wit. The second half of the letter shifts entirely: pure encouragement — nearly word-for-word the same as Letter 41's closing, confirming that Reb Nussun was circulating the same teaching to multiple correspondents that season.

Note on addressee: This letter is addressed to Chaim Nachum — not Yitzchok. The term "my brother" (achi) is a form of spiritual address used for close students.

Key Themes

Hiskalta Aso — You Have Done as Esav The phrase is a sharp biblical allusion: Esav sold his birthright impulsively, forfeiting something of lasting value for an immediate preference. Chaim Nachum's interference — imposing an impossible standard — caused exactly this: a worthy match was lost. The name Esav is not incidental; it is a precise moral diagnosis.
Practical Halochoh and Compassion Reb Nussun's rebuke is legally grounded. The Talmud says sell all you have to marry a daughter to a scholar — but Chaim Nachum has nothing to sell. And the counter-dictum: a grown daughter is like a slave you must free. Idealism that burdens another's household, without personal sacrifice, is not idealism — it is irresponsibility.
The Disrupted Match Reb Nussun reports cryptically that a suitable shiduch came along and was disrupted. The full story, he says, he will tell in person. This is one of the vivid domestic glimpses the letters offer: real people, real consequences, real grief over a lost opportunity.
Uman — The Tziyun This letter is written from Uman — where Rebbe Nachman is buried. Reb Nussun had just arrived safely after a difficult winter journey (described in Letter 43). His being at the tziyun lends the closing words of encouragement a particular weight and authority.
