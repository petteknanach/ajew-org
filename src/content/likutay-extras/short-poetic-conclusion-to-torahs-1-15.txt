# Short Poetic Conclusion to Torahs 1-15

## Conclusion Poem for First Section

תַּם חֵלֶק רִאשׁוֹן
מִשִּׁבְעָה עָשָׂר
דִּבְרֵי נֹעַם
וּמַתַּת חֵן
מִפִּי צַדִּיק
אוֹר יְשָׁרִים
הָאִיר לָנוּ
בְּדֶרֶךְ נָכוֹן
לָלֶכֶת בּוֹ
לִפְנֵי ה'
בְּתָמִים וּבְיֹשֶׁר
בְּשִׂמְחָה וּבְטוּב לֵב
בֶּאֱמוּנָה וּבִתְמִימוּת
בְּתוֹרָה וּבִתְפִלָּה
בְּהִתְבּוֹדְדוּת וּבְשִׂיחָה
בְּזִכָּרוֹן וּבְהַזְכָּרָה
בְּהַכְנָעָה וּבְהִתְבּוֹנְנוּת
בְּהִתְחַזְּקוּת וּבְהִתְעוֹדְדוּת
בְּהַמְשָׁכָה וּבְקִירוּב
בְּתִקּוּן וּבְהַשְׁלָמָה
בְּגִלּוּי וּבְהַסְתָּרָה
בְּהַעֲלָאָה וּבְהוֹרָדָה
בְּחִיּוּת וּבְהִתְעוֹרְרוּת
בְּאַהֲבָה וּבְיִרְאָה
בְּתִקְוָה וּבְצִפִּיָּה
לִישׁוּעָה שְׁלֵמָה
וּלְגְאֻלָּה קְרוֹבָה
בִּמְהֵרָה בְיָמֵינוּ
אָמֵן

## Translation

Completed is the first section
Of seventeen
Words of pleasantness
And gift of grace
From the mouth of the tzaddik
Light of the upright
Has illuminated for us
A straight path
To walk in it
Before God
With wholesomeness and uprightness
With joy and with good heart
With faith and with simplicity
With Torah and with prayer
With seclusion and with conversation
With remembrance and with recollection
With humility and with contemplation
With strengthening and with encouragement
With drawing down and with drawing close
With repair and with completion
With revelation and with concealment
With elevation and with lowering
With vitality and with awakening
With love and with fear
With hope and with anticipation
For complete salvation
And for near redemption
Speedily in our days
Amen

## Commentary

This poetic conclusion appears at the end of the first major section of Likutay Moharan (Torahs 1-15 in some editions, 1-17 in others). It serves as a summary and blessing after completing this initial group of teachings.

### Structure

The poem follows a traditional Hebrew acrostic style, though not strictly alphabetical. It lists the key themes covered in the first section:

1. **The Source** - "From the mouth of the tzaddik"
2. **The Path** - "A straight path to walk in"
3. **The Qualities** - Wholesomeness, uprightness, joy, faith, simplicity
4. **The Practices** - Torah study, prayer, hitbodedut (seclusion)
5. **The Inner Work** - Remembrance, humility, contemplation
6. **The Process** - Strengthening, encouragement, drawing close
7. **The Goal** - Repair, completion, revelation
8. **The Dynamics** - Elevation and lowering, vitality and awakening
9. **The Emotions** - Love and fear, hope and anticipation
10. **The Ultimate Hope** - Complete salvation and redemption

### Themes from Torahs 1-15

The first 15 Torahs of Likutay Moharan establish foundational Breslov concepts:

- **Torah 1** - The power of Torah to elevate grace and importance
- **Torah 2** - The importance of hitbodedut (secluded prayer)
- **Torah 3** - Serving God with simplicity
- **Torah 4** - The value of innovation in Torah study
- **Torah 5** - Prayer before decree (Bachatzotzrot)
- **Torah 6** - Moses and Joshua (connection to the tzaddik)
- **Torah 7** - Faith and the Land of Israel
- **Torah 8** - The menorah of pure gold (defeating the wicked)
- **Torah 9** - "Depths cover them" (exile and redemption)
- **Torah 10** - The sun's tent (illumination through the tzaddik)
- **Torah 11** - The aspect of Rosh Hashanah
- **Torah 12** - The aspect of Chanukah
- **Torah 13** - The aspect of Purim
- **Torah 14** - The aspect of Pesach
- **Torah 15** - The stories of Rabbah bar Bar Chanah

### Liturgical Use

This poem is sometimes recited after studying the first section of Likutay Moharan. It serves as:
1. A summary of what has been learned
2. A prayer for implementation of the teachings
3. A blessing for continued study
4. An expression of hope for redemption

### Historical Context

The division of Likutay Moharan into sections with poetic conclusions may reflect the original oral delivery of the teachings. Rabbi Nachman likely delivered groups of teachings over extended periods, with natural breaks between sections. These poetic conclusions would mark the completion of one cycle and preparation for the next.

### Contemporary Practice

Today, Breslov students might use this poem:
- As a meditation after completing the first section
- As a review of key concepts
- As a prayer for understanding and implementation
- As a connection to the original oral transmission

The poem reminds us that Torah study is not just intellectual acquisition but a transformative process that should lead to practical change in our lives and in the world.
