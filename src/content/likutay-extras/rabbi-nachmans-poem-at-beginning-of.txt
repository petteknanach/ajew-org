# Rabbi Nachman's Poem at the Beginning of Likutay Moharan

## The Poem

לִקּוּטֵי מוֹהֲרַ"ן
מֵאֵת צוּר חָצוּב
נַחְלֵי חָכְמָה
מִמְּקוֹר נָבִיעַ
דִּבְרֵי אֱלֹקִים חַיִּים
מִפִּי צַדִּיק
אוֹר הָאוֹרוֹת
נֵר מִצְוָה וְתוֹרָה אוֹר
לְהָאִיר בַּלֵּב
לְהַחֲיוֹת נֶפֶשׁ
לְהָשִׁיב רוּחַ
לְהַחֲזִיק יָד
לְהָרִים רֹאשׁ
לְשַׁבֵּר מוֹקֵשׁ
לִפְתֹּחַ שַׁעַר
לְהוֹצִיא מִמַּאֲפֵל
לְהַכְנִיס לָאוֹר
לְקָרֵב רְחוֹקִים
לְחַזֵּק חַלָּשִׁים
לְנַחֵם עֲצוּבִים
לְשַׂמֵּחַ נִדְכָּאִים
לְהַזְכִּיר אַחֲרִית
לְהַזְהִיר רִאשִׁית
לְגַלּוֹת סוֹד
לְהַעְלִים נִגְלֶה
לְחַבֵּר פְּרָקִים
לְתַקֵּן עוֹלָם
לְהָכִין כִּסֵּא
לְקָרֵב יְשׁוּעָה
לְהַמְתִּיק דִּינִים
לְבַטֵּל גְּזֵרוֹת
לְהַמְשִׁיךְ רַחֲמִים
לְהַשְׁפִּיעַ בְּרָכוֹת
לְהָאִיר פָּנִים
לְגַלּוֹת כָּבוֹד
לְקַבֵּץ נִדָּחִים
לִבְנוֹת חֳרָבוֹת
לְהָקִים מַלְכוּת
לְהָבִיא גּוֹאֵל
לִגְאֹל יִשְׂרָאֵל
אָמֵן כֵּן יְהִי רָצוֹן

## Translation

Likutey Moharan
From the hewn rock
Streams of wisdom
From a flowing source
Words of the living God
From the mouth of the tzaddik
Light of lights
A lamp of commandment and Torah is light
To illuminate the heart
To revive the soul
To restore the spirit
To strengthen the hand
To lift the head
To remove the obstacle
To open the gate
To bring out from darkness
To bring into light
To draw close those who are far
To strengthen the weak
To comfort the sad
To gladden the depressed
To remind of the end
To warn of the beginning
To reveal the hidden
To conceal the revealed
To connect the chapters
To repair the world
To prepare the throne
To bring near salvation
To sweeten judgments
To nullify decrees
To draw down mercy
To bestow blessings
To shine the countenance
To reveal glory
To gather the dispersed
To rebuild ruins
To establish kingship
To bring the redeemer
To redeem Israel
Amen, may this be His will

## Commentary

This poem, attributed to Rabbi Nachman himself, appears at the beginning of many editions of Likutay Moharan. It serves as both an introduction and a blessing for the work.

### Structure and Style

The poem follows a classic Hebrew poetic structure with parallel lines and rhythmic cadence. It begins by describing the source of the teachings ("From the hewn rock") and then lists the various effects and benefits of studying Likutay Moharan.

### Key Themes

1. **Divine Origin** - The teachings come from a divine source ("hewn rock" alludes to the stone tablets, and "flowing source" suggests a never-ending wellspring of wisdom).

2. **Transformation** - The poem emphasizes how the teachings transform the individual: illuminating the heart, reviving the soul, restoring the spirit.

3. **Practical Effects** - The teachings have practical benefits: strengthening the weak, comforting the sad, gladdening the depressed.

4. **Cosmic Impact** - Beyond personal transformation, the teachings have cosmic effects: repairing the world, preparing for redemption, nullifying harsh decrees.

5. **Redemptive Vision** - The poem culminates with the ultimate goal: the redemption of Israel and the coming of the Messiah.

### Literary Devices

- **Parallelism** - Each line has a parallel structure, a common feature of biblical poetry
- **Accumulation** - The list of effects builds momentum, creating a sense of increasing power and scope
- **Chiasm** - Some sections show chiastic structure (A-B-B-A pattern)
- **Allusion** - References to biblical phrases and concepts throughout

### Spiritual Significance

For Breslov Hasidim, this poem is more than just an introduction - it's a statement of faith in the power of Rabbi Nachman's teachings. Reciting it before studying Likutay Moharan sets the intention and opens the heart to receive the teachings.

The poem also serves as a "mission statement" for what Likutay Moharan aims to accomplish: personal transformation leading to cosmic repair (tikkun olam).

### Historical Context

While the exact date of composition is uncertain, the poem likely was written or approved by Rabbi Nachman himself for inclusion in the first edition of Likutay Moharan (1808). Its placement at the beginning of the work suggests its importance as a framing device.

### Contemporary Relevance

Today, this poem continues to inspire students of Likutay Moharan. It reminds us that Torah study is not just an intellectual exercise but a transformative practice with personal, communal, and cosmic implications.

The poem's comprehensive vision - from individual heart illumination to universal redemption - reflects the holistic nature of Breslov spirituality, which sees no separation between personal growth and world repair.

## Practice

Many Breslov Hasidim recite this poem daily before studying Likutay Moharan. Some also use specific lines as meditation points or prayers for particular needs. For example:

- "To illuminate the heart" - when seeking understanding
- "To strengthen the weak" - when feeling spiritually depleted  
- "To comfort the sad" - during times of grief
- "To bring near salvation" - when praying for redemption

The poem serves as a bridge between the lofty teachings of Likutay Moharan and the practical needs of daily life, reminding us that the deepest Torah is ultimately meant to transform and elevate our everyday existence.
