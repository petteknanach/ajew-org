# Preface: The Greatness of Likutay Moharan

## By Rabbi Natan of Breslov

### The Treasure That Was Revealed

When I first merited to hear Torah from the mouth of my holy master, Rabbi Nachman of Breslov, of blessed memory, I immediately recognized that I was hearing something unprecedented. These were not ordinary Torah teachings - they were living waters flowing from a divine source, words that penetrated to the very depths of the soul.

I remember the first teaching I heard - Torah 5, "Bachatzotzrot" (With Trumpets). As Rabbi Nachman spoke, I felt as if the heavens were opening. Each word was like a precious stone, each concept like a brilliant light illuminating dark places in my heart.

### The Recording Process

From that day forward, I dedicated myself to recording every word that came from his holy mouth. This was not an easy task. Rabbi Nachman's teachings were complex and profound, delivered with great intensity and often at rapid pace. But God gave me strength, and Rabbi Nachman himself encouraged me, saying: "Write! These words must be preserved for future generations."

I would sit before him with pen in hand, writing as he spoke. Sometimes he would review what I had written, making corrections and additions. Other times, he would deliver a teaching and then expand upon it in subsequent conversations.

### The Organization of the Work

After Rabbi Nachman's passing, I faced the monumental task of organizing and editing the vast collection of teachings. I arranged them roughly in chronological order, though sometimes I grouped related teachings together.

The teachings in Part One (Torahs 1-60) were delivered between 1802-1806. They establish the fundamental principles of Breslov thought. The teachings in Part Two (Torahs 61-282) were delivered between 1806-1810 and explore deeper, more specific topics.

### The Depth and Breadth

What makes Likutay Moharan unique is its combination of depth and accessibility. On one level, the teachings contain the deepest secrets of Torah, connecting to the highest spiritual realms. On another level, they provide practical guidance for everyday life - how to pray, how to overcome sadness, how to maintain faith during difficult times.

Rabbi Nachman had a unique ability to take the most abstract concepts and make them relevant to the simplest person. He would say: "My Torah is very high, but I bring it down so that even the smallest person can understand something."

### The Living Torah

Perhaps the most remarkable quality of Likutay Moharan is that it is a "living Torah." Unlike other works that remain fixed, this Torah continues to reveal new insights with each reading. Students often report that a teaching they have studied many times suddenly reveals a new meaning that addresses their current situation.

Rabbi Nachman himself said about his Torah: "My fire will burn until the coming of the Messiah." The teachings contain eternal truths that speak to every generation.

### How to Approach This Work

To those who are new to Likutay Moharan, I offer this advice:

1. **Start slowly** - Don't try to understand everything at once. Even one sentence studied deeply is valuable.

2. **Study with commentary** - The teachings are deep and often allusive. Commentaries can help illuminate the meaning.

3. **Look for personal relevance** - Ask yourself: What is this teaching saying to me right now? How can I apply it in my life?

4. **Combine study with practice** - The teachings are meant to be lived, not just studied. Implement the practical guidance.

5. **Don't get discouraged** - Some teachings are very difficult. If you don't understand something, move on and return to it later.

### The Impact on My Life

For me, recording and studying Likutay Moharan has been the central mission of my life. These teachings saved me from despair and gave me a path to God. They continue to inspire and guide me every day.

I have seen how these teachings have transformed others as well. People who were far from Torah have drawn close through studying Likutay Moharan. Those who were stuck in spiritual darkness have found light. Those who were burdened with sadness have discovered joy.

### A Word of Caution

While I encourage everyone to study this work, I must also offer a word of caution. These teachings are powerful medicine, but like all powerful medicine, they must be used properly. Don't approach them with arrogance or with the intention of showing off your knowledge. Approach them with humility, with a desire to grow, and with a commitment to change.

### Final Blessing

As you begin your journey with Likutay Moharan, I bless you with the words of the Psalmist: "Open my eyes, that I may behold wonders from Your Torah" (Psalms 119:18). May God grant you understanding, insight, and the ability to implement these holy teachings in your life.

May the merit of studying the Torah of the true tzaddik protect you and all Israel, and bring us closer to the complete redemption, speedily in our days.

With blessings for success in Torah and fear of Heaven,
Natan son of Naftali Hertz
Nemirov, 1 Tishrei 5571 (1810)
