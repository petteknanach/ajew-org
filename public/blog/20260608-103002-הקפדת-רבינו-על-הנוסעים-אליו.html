הקפדת רבינו על הנוסעים אליו?

Rabbainu's Seeming Severity Toward His Closest Followers

ב"ה

חיי מוהר"ן רסג: פעם אחת ביום הושענא רבא אמר בפני העולם: ומה אעשה ששני רוצחים עומדים עלי ואומרים אף על פי כן שאני מנהיג (שקורין גיטער יוד), והראה בידו על רבי יודל ורבי שמואל אייזיק בעת שאמר "שני רוצחים" — היינו שעליהם היה נתכוון, מחמת שהם היו כרוכים אחריו מאד וכו' ע"כ.

עיין באבניה ברזל, שיחות וספורים מרבנו ז"ל אות מא — השיחה 'נוקם ונוטר כנחש' נאמרה בוואך נאכט (לילה שלפני הברית) של הילד ר' יעקב ז"ל. ואר הברית כשבאו אליו אנשי שלומנו אמר להם רבנו זצוק"ל: נאך וואס וכו' — מדוע אתם נוסעים אלי, אני שונא אתכם, ע"כ. (בשיח שרפי קודש ח-תקעה כתוב שמעולם לא נשמע דבר כזה וכו').

ויש לציין עוד למש"כ לעיל חיי מוהר"ן אות ו' — ובתחלה הקפיד קצת ואמר שאנו עושים לעצמנו שבתים, ע"ש.

English Translation & Commentary

Chayey Moharan §263: Once on Hoshana Rabbah, Rabbainu said before the congregation: "What can I do — two murderers are standing over me and nevertheless saying that I am a leader (a 'good Jew')." He gestured toward R' Yudel and R' Shmuel Isaac when he said "two murderers." They were the ones he intended — because they were extremely devoted to him, constantly clinging to him.

Eveniah Barzel, Sippurim §41: The talk titled "Nokem V'Noter K'Nachash" ("Avenger and Bearer of Grudge Like a Snake") was delivered on the Vach Nacht (the night before the circumcision) of the child R' Yaakov z"l. At the bris when his followers came to him, Rabbainu said: "Nu — after what are you traveling to me? I hate you!" (In Sich Sarfei Kodesh it is written that such a thing was never heard before.)

The post adds: See also Chayey Moharan §6 — at first, Rabbainu was somewhat strict and said that we were building ourselves "Sabbath houses," as described there.

🧠 The Core Insight

These incidents are shocking on the surface. Rabbainu, the embodiment of love and compassion, calls his closest followers "murderers" and tells them "I hate you." What does this mean?

The answer is in the nature of the Rebbe-talmid relationship at its highest level. When a person comes to the Tzaddik for personal benefit — to receive something, to feel good, to build their own spiritual "house" — they are, in a sense, "murdering" the relationship. They are using the Tzaddik rather than surrendering to him.

R' Yudel and R' Shmuel Isaac were the most devoted chassidim — they were always with Rabbainu. But Rabbainu called them "murderers" not to insult them, but to reveal the highest standard: one must come to the Tzaddik with complete nullification — not for what you can get, but to lose yourself entirely.

The "I hate you" on the Vach Nacht is even more extreme. Why would Rabbainu say this? Because the chassidim came for the celebration, for the simcha, for the spiritual experience. Rabbainu was testing them: Will you travel to me even when I tell you I hate you? Will you come when there's nothing for your ego to grasp, when all that's left is the pure will to be with the Tzaddik regardless of what you receive?

This aligns with Chayey Moharan §6: at first, people come and build themselves "Sabbath houses" — they come for their own spiritual enrichment. Rabbainu's strictness was meant to break this, to elevate the relationship from "what I get" to "who I am with."

The true chassid travels to the Tzaddik because he must — not because it's pleasant, not because it elevates him, but because his soul cannot do otherwise. Even when told "I hate you," he answers: "I'm still coming."

Great blessings of Na Nach Nachma Nachman Me'Uman!
